A buckling-constrained example

This page walks through the buckling-constrained truss design that the test suite uses to compare IPMAlg with AugLagAlg — a nonconvex semidefinite program with a single global buckling constraint.

The problem

A statically indeterminate three-bar truss: the nodes A = (0,0), B = (1,0) and D = (0.5,-1) are fixed, the tip C = (0.5,1) is free (two displacement degrees of freedom), and the bars A–C, B–C, D–C have cross-sectional areas x = (x₁, x₂, x₃) and unit modulus E = 1. A reference load P = (0,-1) acts at the tip. The design problem is

\[minimize \quad \sum_i l_i x_i \quad s.t. \quad S(x) := K(x) + \bar{c}\, G(x, u(x)) \succeq 0, \qquad x \ge x_{\min},\]

where l_i are the bar lengths, \bar{c} = 10 the required load factor, and the buckling constraint is one global semidefinite constraint (no eigenvalue aggregation is used).

The global buckling LMI

The elastic stiffness matrix is the design-weighted assembly of the bar stiffnesses,

\[K(x) = \sum_i \frac{x_i}{l_i} \begin{bmatrix} c_{x,i}^2 & c_{x,i} c_{y,i} \\ c_{x,i} c_{y,i} & c_{y,i}^2 \end{bmatrix},\]

and the displacement solves K(x) u = P. The geometric (stress) stiffness comes from the axial forces N_i = x_i (u_b - u_a)·c_i / l_i — negative in compression:

\[G(x, u) = \sum_i \frac{N_i}{l_i} \begin{bmatrix} c_{y,i}^2 & -c_{x,i} c_{y,i} \\ -c_{x,i} c_{y,i} & c_{x,i}^2 \end{bmatrix}.\]

The generalized eigenvalue problem K v = λ G v describes the buckling modes: a negative G direction means the compressive stress softens the corresponding stiffness mode, and the structure buckles when the softest mode reaches zero — λ_min(K, G) ≥ \bar{c} ⟺ K + \bar{c} G ⪰ 0 (with G evaluated at the reference-load displacement). Because the truss is statically indeterminate, the force distribution — and hence G — depends on the design, which makes the LMI genuinely nonconvex in x. (A statically determinate truss would have design-independent forces and the constraint would collapse to a linear bound.)

The unconstrained volume minimum x → x_min violates the LMI: thin bars carry the same compressive force at vanishing stiffness, so the buckling eigenvalue is negative. Thickening the bars restores it — at the optimum the LMI is exactly active (the cheapest design sits on the buckling boundary).

Solving

using NonconvexInteriorPoint, NonconvexSemidefinite

# the truss constants and the buckling constraint map
buckling_state(x) = begin
    K = foldl(+, [
        x[i] .* ([c[1]^2 c[1]*c[2]; c[1]*c[2] c[2]^2] ./ L[i]) for
        (i, c) in enumerate(dirs)
    ])
    u = K \ P
    G = foldl(+, [
        ((x[i] * dot(u, dirs[i]) / L[i]) / L[i]) .*
        [c[2]^2 -c[1]*c[2]; -c[1]*c[2] c[1]^2] for
        (i, c) in enumerate(dirs)
    ])
    return K + cbar .* G, u
end

m = Model(x -> sum(L .* x))
addvar!(m, fill(0.01, 3), fill(50.0, 3))
add_set_constraint!(m, x -> buckling_state(x)[1], PSDCone(2))

# dense oracle (default)
r = optimize(m, IPMAlg(), [25.0, 25.0, 5.0])

# matrix-free oracle (label-selected: no dense PSD assembly)
m = Model(x -> sum(L .* x))
addvar!(m, fill(0.01, 3), fill(50.0, 3))
add_set_constraint!(m, x -> buckling_state(x)[1], PSDCone(2); flags = Set([:mf]))
r = optimize(
    m, IPMAlg(), [25.0, 25.0, 5.0];
    options = IPMOptions(
        evaluation = IPMEvaluationSelection(matrixfree_all = true),
    ),
)

Both the dense and the matrix-free oracle converge from a strictly feasible start (the PSD barrier requires S(x₀) ≻ 0) to the same design, and AugLagAlg agrees:

algorithmminimizervolumeλ_min(S(x*))
IPMAlg (dense)(22.36, 22.36, 0.01)50.020.0
IPMAlg (matrix-free)(22.36, 22.36, 0.01)50.020.0

The symmetric optimum puts all the material into the two inclined bars (x₃ at its lower bound: the vertical bar carries only tension and does not drive buckling), and the smallest buckling eigenvalue is exactly zero — the classic "optimal design sits on the stability boundary" signature of this problem class (Kočvara–Stingl PBM).

The full version of this example, including the comparison with AugLagAlg, lives in test/buckling.jl. The trace-estimated oracle is deliberately not used here: its log-determinant estimate is only meaningful for positive definite matrices, and the iterates of this problem approach the buckling boundary where S is singular.
